# 5 English Grammar Mistakes You Might Make Every Day
### Master Production Script

**Working title:** English Grammar for Beginners: 5 Mistakes You Make Every Day
**Runtime:** ~5:40
**Format:** 2D animated explainer with recurring characters (Alex & Maya)
**Audience:** Beginner–intermediate English learners
**Style:** Flat vector cartoon · big readable captions · speech bubbles · red/green color coding
**Global UI:** A 5-slot "Mistake Tracker" fills green as each of the five mistakes is corrected.

> How to read this script: **VO** = narrator voice-over, **Alex/Maya** = character dialogue,
> **ON-SCREEN** = text that appears on screen, **SFX/MUSIC** = sound cues, **PACING** = timing notes.

---

## 1. Opening hook — 0:00–0:15

**VO**
"Do you say, 'I am agree,' 'She don't like it,' or 'I didn't went'? These mistakes are more common than you think. In the next few minutes, you'll learn five simple corrections that can make your English sound much more natural. And number five — is a mistake you might make every single day."

**ON-SCREEN (fast cuts, ~2–3s each)**
- 0:00–0:03 — Big red title: `5 ENGLISH MISTAKES YOU MIGHT MAKE`
- 0:03–0:06 — `I AM AGREE` (red) → flash → `I AGREE` (green, partial)
- 0:06–0:09 — `She DON'T like it` (red) → flash → `She DOESN'T like it` (green, partial)
- 0:09–0:12 — `I DIDN'T WENT` (red) → flash → `I DIDN'T GO` (green, partial)
- 0:12–0:15 — `#5 = a mistake you make EVERY day` with a "hmm?" wiggle

**VISUAL** — A confident cartoon character delivers the wrong lines. A second character freezes mid-motion (comic stop-frame), eyes wide, sweat drop.

**SFX/MUSIC** — Upbeat, curious theme in. Each wrong line gets a "buzz" + red flash; each tease gets a soft "ding."

**PACING** — Hard cuts every 2–3s. Do not explain the corrections here — only tease them.

---

## 2. Set up the challenge — 0:15–0:35

**VO**
"Meet Alex. Alex knows lots of English words — but he keeps making five small grammar mistakes. His friend Maya thinks she can catch them all. Can you spot the mistakes before Maya does? Let's find out."

**VISUAL** — Intro sting. Alex waves; Maya appears with a magnifying glass and a "spot it!" pose. Name cards: `ALEX` / `MAY`. Show the empty 5-slot Mistake Tracker.

**ON-SCREEN** — `Can YOU catch all 5?`

**SFX/MUSIC** — Theme continues; playful "let's go" sting on the title card.

**PACING** — Give viewers a reason to follow the story, not feel lectured.

---

## 3. Mistake one — 0:35–1:20

**Wrong:** `I am agree` ❌ → **Right:** `I agree` ✅

**Alex:** "I am agree with you!"
**May:** "Wait! Just say, 'I agree with you.'"

**Explanation (VO):** "Agree is already a verb. We don't need 'am' before it. 'I agree' — clean and simple."

**ON-SCREEN** — `I am agree` (red) → `I agree` (green). Rule card: `Agree = verb. No "am".`

**Quick test — pause 2s so viewers can answer:**
"Which is correct: 'I agree' or 'I am agree'?"
- Countdown 2s → reveal: `I agree` ✅

**VISUAL** — Alex nods and says it correctly; May gives a thumbs-up. Tracker slot 1 turns green.

**SFX/MUSIC** — Buzz on the wrong line, ding on the fix, tiny "ding-ding" on the correct answer.

---

## 4. Mistake two — 1:20–2:05

**Wrong:** `She don't` ❌ → **Right:** `She doesn't` ✅

**Alex:** "She don't play football."
**May:** "She doesn't play football!"

**Explanation (VO):** "With he, she, and it, we use 'doesn't' in the present simple negative — not 'don't.'"

**ON-SCREEN examples**
- `He doesn't run.`
- `It doesn't work.`

**Retention device** — Alex repeats the wrong sentence, then corrects it, with a funny spring/whoosh SFX on the correction.

**VISUAL** — The character morphs `don't` → `doesn't` (the extra "es" pops in with a bounce). Tracker slot 2 turns green.

**SFX/MUSIC** — Buzz, then the funny correction SFX, then ding.

---

## 5. Mistake three — 2:05–2:50

**Wrong:** `I didn't went` ❌ → **Right:** `I didn't go` ✅

**Explanation (VO):** "After 'didn't,' always use the base form of the verb: go, eat, see — never went, ate, saw."

**Mini-quiz (countdown):** "I didn't ___ breakfast. Eat or ate?"
- 3-2-1 countdown → visual transform `ate` → `eat` → reveal: "I didn't eat breakfast."

**VISUAL** — Show the base-form family (go / eat / see) with the past forms fading away. Tracker slot 3 turns green.

**SFX/MUSIC** — Ticking countdown, "transform" shimmer, ding.

---

## 6. Mistake four — 2:50–3:35

**Wrong:** `I have 25 years` ❌ → **Right:** `I am 25 years old` ✅

**Explanation (VO — keep under 15s):** "In English we talk about age with 'be,' not 'have.' 'I am 25 years old.' 'She is 30.'"

**ON-SCREEN examples**
- `I am 20.`
- `She is 30 years old.`

**VISUAL** — Alex meets someone new; the numbers pop as he says his age correctly. Tracker slot 4 turns green.

**SFX/MUSIC** — Soft "age pop" plink, ding.

---

## 7. Mistake five — 3:35–4:25 — FINAL BIG REVEAL

**Wrong:** `He go to school every day` ❌ → **Right:** `He goes to school every day` ✅

**Explanation (VO):** "In the present simple, we add -s or -es to most verbs with he, she, or it."

**ON-SCREEN examples**
- `She works.`
- `He watches TV.`
- `It rains.`

**Retention device** — Alex stumbles and asks viewers to help.
- Prompt: `Help Alex! He ___ to school.`
- Viewers shout the answer. Then Alex says it correctly, with celebration.
- Tracker slot 5 turns green → **all five slots light up.**

**VISUAL** — Big reveal moment; the characters celebrate.

**SFX/MUSIC** — Building tension, then a triumphant celebration sting when Alex nails it.

---

## 8. Rapid-fire challenge — 4:25–5:15

**VISUAL** — Scoreboard + ticking clock; 3 quick questions.

**Q1:** `She don't understand.` → **`She doesn't understand.`**
**Q2:** `I didn't saw him.` → **`I didn't see him.`**
**Q3:** `He play football every day.` → **`He plays football every day.`**

**PACING** — Each question 3–4s. Reveal answers one at a time. Celebrate correct answers; prompt viewers to share their score.

**ON-SCREEN** — After all three: `How many did you get? Share your score!`

**SFX/MUSIC** — Tick-tock clock; ding per correct answer; mini fanfare at the end.

---

## 9. Strong closing CTA — 5:15–5:40

**VO**
"How many did you get right — three, four, or all five? Tell me your score in the comments! If you want to learn English grammar through simple animated stories, subscribe and join us for the next lesson. And remember: making mistakes is part of learning. The important thing is to keep practising!"

**VISUAL** — Alex says all five sentences correctly in a fast montage; May gives a thumbs-up. Subscribe prompt + next-video teaser: `Say These 10 English Sentences Correctly!`

**ON-SCREEN** — Subscribe button · `Next: Say These 10 English Sentences Correctly!`

**SFX/MUSIC** — Celebratory outro theme; soft subscribe "pop."

## Mistake quick-reference

| # | Wrong | Correct | Rule |
|---|-------|---------|------|
| 1 | I am agree | I agree | "Agree" is a verb — no "am" |
| 2 | She don't | She doesn't | he/she/it → "doesn't" (present simple negative) |
| 3 | I didn't went | I didn't go | after "didn't," use the base form |
| 4 | I have 25 years | I am 25 years old | age uses "be," not "have" |
| 5 | He go to school | He goes to school | he/she/it → add -s/-es (present simple) |

## Retention devices used
1. Teased corrections in the hook, explained later.
2. 2s pause + quick test (mistake 1).
3. Funny SFX on repeat-and-correct (mistake 2).
4. Countdown + visual transform (mistake 3).
5. Viewer-help reveal (mistake 5).
6. Rapid-fire scoreboard + score-sharing CTA.
